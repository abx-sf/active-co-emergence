The Constraint of Interpretation

Part of the research sequence — Activation

What appears does not coincide with what is understood.

They are connected.

When they operate, they constitute a single process: attention exposes, interpretation articulates.

They do not simply follow one another.

They hold.

But this connection is not guaranteed.

It can alter.

—

Attention is not neutral.

It is already oriented, selective, crossed by conditions.

However, it is not yet closed.

It holds an opening.

It does not produce meaning.

It does not stabilize.

It exposes to a field.

Meaning is not contained in things.

It is not something to extract.

It does not precede.

It is co-emergent.

An effect of the field when the field activates.

It is not stable.

It is not available in advance.

—

Interpretation intervenes here.

It does not create from nothing, nor simply add.

It organizes what emerges.

It brings it toward a form.

Toward meaning.

Meaning is not arbitrary.

It is constrained.

It depends on the conditions of the field, on what has effectively taken form.

It is not free, but neither definitive.

—

The critical point is not the stabilization of meaning.

It is its closure.

When meaning presents itself as already given, not as an outcome but as a starting point, the field is no longer activated.

Attention contracts.

Meaning has no way to emerge operationally.

It does not disappear, but it does not operate.

—

In this condition, interpretation changes function.

It no longer responds to what appears.

It is no longer constrained nor exposed.

It applies.

It mobilizes available meaning, already formed categories, ready lexicons.

Here the shift occurs.

Interpretation, not regulated, becomes projection.

—

Projection is not an occasional error.

It is a regime.

It does not construct meaning.

It anticipates it.

It does not pass through the field.

It replaces it.

It operates.

For this reason, it is difficult to detect.

It does not present itself as a distortion, but as evidence.

—

The difference between interpretation and projection is not quantitative.

It is not “more” or “less” interpretation.

It is a difference of constraint.

Interpretation remains exposed to what appears.

Projection does not.

—

In many contemporary practices, this difference tends to no longer operate.

Not because there is too much interpretation, but because interpretation is no longer forced to respond.

Meaning is already available.

Meaning does not emerge.

Attention is not crossed, but bypassed.

—

In this context, critical practice does not disappear.

It reduces.

Verification is not required.

A plausible formulation is sufficient.

The difference between reading and attributing attenuates.

—

There is no gaze without projection.

There is no guarantee.

The question is not to eliminate projection, but to recognize its activation.

To hold the constraint.

To expose interpretation to what might not confirm it.

—

When this does not occur, seeing, understanding, attributing coincide.

The process closes.

Everything is readable.

Everything is immediately available.

And for this very reason, everything loses necessity.

—

The problem is not the presence of meaning.

But its autonomy from the field.

When meaning is no longer forced to emerge, but can simply be applied, meaning no longer has a function.

And without this function, attention is no longer required.
